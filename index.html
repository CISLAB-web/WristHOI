Abstract

Capturing the “last mile” of interaction

Fine-grained hand-object interaction (HOI) understanding relies heavily on the precise capture of contact dynamics. However, conventional allocentric and egocentric viewpoints are often hindered by severe self-occlusion and object-induced occlusion, resulting in sparse and fragmented contact cues. We present WristHOI, a dual wrist-mounted dataset designed to capture the proximal “last-mile” of interaction. WristHOI employs a symmetric near-field camera configuration to provide synchronized, complementary observations from both dorsal and palmar perspectives. While this near-field setup introduces technical hurdles such as extreme perspective distortions and rapid viewpoint variations, it offers unprecedented visual evidence at the fingertip-object interface. The dataset comprises 1,260 sequences of 10 subjects interacting with 14 objects across three grasping paradigms (G2, G3, G5). In total, WristHOI provides 1.45M frames with high-quality annotations at multiple levels, including hand-object poses, contact labels, and temporal interaction states. We establish a comprehensive benchmark for pose estimation, contact prediction, and temporal interaction understanding. Our results demonstrate the value of wrist-mounted observations for capturing fine-grained interaction cues under severe occlusion.

What's inside

Rich, multi-level annotations

Hand and object poses live in one world frame and reproject consistently into every view.

01

Nine synchronized RGB-D views

Seven fixed allocentric cameras surround the workspace, while two wrist-mounted cameras look at the hand from the dorsal and palmar side at close range. Hand (MANO) and object labels live in one world frame and reproject consistently into every view.

02

2D hand keypoints & masks

21 hand keypoints are provided in every view, together with hand segmentation masks for the wrist cameras, where extreme perspective and near-field distortion make the hand appear very differently from conventional viewpoints.

03

14 everyday objects

Scanned object meshes at metric scale — from bottles, cups and cans to tools such as pliers, a stapler and a glue gun — with a rigid 6-DoF object pose in every frame, grasped under three paradigms (G2, G3, G5).

04

Dense hand–object contact

Per-vertex contact on the 778 MANO vertices is derived from the 3D hand and object geometry, and can be visualized directly in the wrist views where the fingertip–object interface is actually observed.

05

Four-phase interaction states

Each sequence is segmented into Approach Contact-Start In-Contact Release, and every phase is paired with a language description — enabling text-guided temporal localization.

Wrist-camera localization

Where are the wrist cameras?

The wrist cameras move with the hand. Their world pose is recovered for every frame from a static AprilTag map, so that world-space hand and object labels become pixel-aligned in the egocentric wrist views.

  1. Tag mapAprilTags on the workspace are refined by multi-view reprojection in the fixed cameras.
  2. DetectionTags visible in each wrist view are detected per frame.
  3. Joint PnPAll visible tag corners constrain one camera pose (RANSAC + LM).
  4. Labels in wrist viewsHand and object labels are reprojected into the dorsal and palmar views.

Contact distance threshold sensitivity (τ = 1–5 mm)

WristHOI marks a MANO vertex as in contact when its hand–object distance falls below an on-threshold τ (baseline τ = 4 mm, with hysteresis in the released pipeline). Using cached per-vertex distances, we re-run the same classification logic at τ ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4, 5} mm and report: Contact ratio — the average fraction of hand vertices labeled in contact; Flip rate — the fraction of frames whose contact labeling differs from the τ = 4 mm baseline. Flip rates stay low near the baseline (e.g. 2.9% at τ = 3, 3.2% at τ = 5), indicating stable automatic contact labels. This is not a per-state breakdown; see the Temporal tab for how the four interaction states separate in distance.

Manual validation uses three-annotator fused ground truth on large-scale subsets: 2D and 3D hand pose on 1,744 frames (3D: dual-view, 1,744 × 2); contact labels on 1,260 frames.

Automatic checks (object depth, penetration, temporal states) use full p007+p008 sequences (5,014 / 5,733 / full-sequence frame counts, respectively).

These results provide quantitative evidence supporting the reliability of WristHOI annotations. Contact labels are validated through complementary evaluations of hand pose accuracy, object pose quality, geometric consistency, physical plausibility, temporal state separation, and manual verification.
